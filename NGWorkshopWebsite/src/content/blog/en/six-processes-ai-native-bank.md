A corporate KYB review averages $2,598 and 95 days (Fenergo). That's a global figure, but anyone running onboarding at an Israeli bank knows the feeling: long processes, repeated document requests, and customers who drop out halfway through.

The question every bank CTO is asking today isn't "should we adopt AI" - it's "where do we start without blowing up regulatory control". The answer isn't "everywhere at once." Camunda recently published an analysis of six banking processes where AI agents are already proving their value - and every one of them maps directly to Israeli banking realities.

## 1. Customer onboarding and KYC/KYB

Agents collect identity data, validate it against policy, request missing documents, and suggest suitable products - while an analyst still makes the final risk call. Mauritius Commercial Bank cut onboarding time by 64% this way, and AI adoption in KYC/AML jumped from 42% to 82% in a single year.

A bank operating under Bank of Israel supervision has to document every onboarding step: who reviewed it, against which policy, and when it was approved. An agent running inside an orchestrated process, rather than as a standalone chatbot, delivers exactly that - every step is logged, and the final decision stays with a person.

## 2. Lending document processing and income verification

Classification and extraction agents sort documents, pull income data, and flag gaps. Harmoney cut decision time from four days to under 18 minutes this way, with a 20-30% drop in abandonment for applicants with complex income.

This is a process where most files are "clean" and can flow straight through, while a human underwriter only sees the exceptions - with full context to decide quickly.

## 3. Payment exceptions and investigations

Agents parse ISO 20022 messages, enrich records, spot duplicates, and propose resolutions - while an audit trail stays intact across all counterparties involved. Swift estimates the shift to ISO 20022 can cut resolution times by up to 80% and save the industry roughly $600 million a year, with a compliance deadline set for November 2026.

For Israeli banks connected to cross-border payment rails, this is a process where speed and documentation have to arrive together - exactly where process orchestration makes the difference.

## 4. Fraud detection and AML alert triage

Multiple agents score risk, gather evidence, and draft a rationale in parallel - but the final call stays with a human investigator. Over 70% of firms report false-positive rates above 30%, and Deloitte projects generative-AI-enabled fraud losses could reach $40 billion by 2027. NatWest saves 21 minutes per case this way.

This is perhaps the most compliance-sensitive process in Israeli banking: every AML alert needs to remain explainable and auditable, even when AI assists with initial screening.

## 5. Financial-crime investigation and SAR filing

Agents assemble evidence, reconstruct fund flows, and draft a report - while a human investigator directs the case and signs off. A single SAR takes 4-8 hours and consumes 30-40% of compliance hours at mid-market firms, against a 30-day regulatory clock. Celent found agentic AI can cut AML investigation time by 60%.

This is a process where speed matters, but legal accountability stays unambiguously with a person - which is exactly why it needs a clear process framework that logs every step.

## 6. Credit decisioning and underwriting

Agents run risk models, reconcile data, and return a recommendation with rationale - but an underwriter decides. Under the EU AI Act, credit scoring falls into the "high-risk" category, and its obligations start applying in August 2026. Well-implemented orchestration improved analyst productivity by 20-60%, and in one EY partnership, analysts handled seven times more cases with manual effort down 86%.

In Israel, any AI-assisted underwriting model needs to be explainable - a requirement that keeps strengthening under banking supervision as well.

## What these six processes have in common

In every one, the agent doesn't "decide alone." It operates inside an orchestrated process with clear boundaries: what it's allowed to do, when it stops for human approval, and how every step is logged. That's exactly the difference between an impressive AI experiment and a process you can run under Bank of Israel supervision.

## What this means for your organization

A bank that wants to be AI-native doesn't need a giant "AI project." It needs to pick one process from this list, build it as an orchestrated business process with human-in-the-loop at the right points, and measure a business outcome - not just "did the agent work."
