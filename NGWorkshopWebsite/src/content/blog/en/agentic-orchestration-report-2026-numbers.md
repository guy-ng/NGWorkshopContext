73% of organizations admit there's a significant gap between their vision for agentic AI and what's actually happening. That's not a guess - it's a finding from Camunda's 2026 State of Agentic Orchestration & Automation report, which surveyed IT and business decision-makers worldwide. Here are five numbers from that report, and what each one means for an Israeli enterprise planning its next AI move.

## 1. 73% - the vision-reality gap

Nearly three out of four organizations report a significant gap between what they hoped to achieve with agentic AI and where they actually stand. This is the number that frames the entire report: good intentions, budget in place, but results on the ground that fall short of the deck presented to leadership.

**What this means for your organization:** if your agentic AI project feels like it "hasn't taken off" the way it was planned, you're not alone - and it isn't necessarily a sign of project failure. It's a sign the gap sits exactly where you'd expect, and there's a systematic way to close it.

## 2. 71% experiment, only 11% reached production

71% of organizations report they're already using AI agents. But only 11% managed to get a single AI agent into a live production environment over the past year. The gap between these two numbers is essentially the "automation ceiling" the report describes.

**What this means for your organization:** be skeptical of any "successful pilot" presented to you - the right question isn't "did it work in the demo" but "what will it take for this to work at scale, with real data and real customers."

## 3. 84% fear business risk without IT controls

84% of organizations cite business risk from running AI in processes without proper IT controls. An additional 80% cite a lack of transparency about how AI is actually used in their business processes, and 66% raise regulatory and compliance concerns.

**What this means for your organization:** for an Israeli enterprise under regulatory supervision - whether Bank of Israel guidance, capital markets regulation, or privacy law - the question "who is accountable if the agent gets it wrong" needs to be answered *before* launch, not after. Business risk without IT controls is exactly where process orchestration makes the difference: it adds a deterministic control layer around the flexible AI component.

## 4. 48% run AI agents in isolated silos

Nearly half of organizations currently run AI agents as isolated components - not as part of an end-to-end business process. The agent solves a point task, but nobody sees the full picture: what happened before the agent stepped in, and what happens after it finishes.

**What this means for your organization:** a brilliant AI agent sitting outside the process doesn't improve organizational efficiency - it just adds another disconnection point. We've written before about why AI agents need a conductor for exactly this reason: an agent's most important capability isn't what it can do alone, but how it connects to the steps before and after it.

## 5. 90% agree: AI needs orchestration like any other automated endpoint

90% of the IT leaders surveyed recognize that AI needs to be orchestrated just like any other automated endpoint in the organization - not run separately. An additional 88% agree that orchestrating across processes maximizes the return on AI investment.

**What this means for your organization:** this is the bright spot in the report. The direction is clear to most decision-makers - the only question is who moves from recognition to execution first. That's exactly the shift we described in our 90-day orchestration roadmap: you don't need to rebuild everything, you need a phased plan that shows results early.

## What this means for your organization, overall

These five numbers tell one story: the problem isn't a lack of will or budget, it's the absence of an operating structure that connects AI to the business process, to control, and to accountability. An Israeli enterprise that wants to avoid staying in the majority that never reaches production needs to build the orchestration and governance layer *before* expanding its use of AI agents - not after.
