Every process in your enterprise is legacy. Not because it is old, but because it was designed in a world without AI. That is Camunda's claim in its own blog, and it is why "process re-engineering" is getting a new meaning in 2026.

A CTO building the 2027 budget right now knows the pattern: migrating from an old BPMS to Camunda takes months, costs money, and ends with the exact same processes running on newer infrastructure. Camunda argues the next step looks completely different, and it is called ProcessOS.

## The problem: migration is not improvement

The average enterprise runs over 500 core processes. Traditional re-engineering of a single process used to take about a year - workshops, interviews with process owners, manual mapping, then implementation. At that scale, you cannot improve all 500 processes. In practice, most organizations migrate a process "as is" and hope the new platform will be enough.

The problem is the underlying assumption: migration treats the existing structure as valid. Real re-engineering asks a different question - should this task exist at all, and in what form, now that AI can perform part of it.

## What ProcessOS actually is

ProcessOS is Camunda's intelligence layer that accompanies a process through four capabilities: discovery, re-engineering, building, and continuous optimization. The real difference from earlier BPM tooling is that it does not look at a process once at the start of a project - it comes back to it every time the business changes.

In the Quote-to-Cash pilot Camunda describes, cycle time dropped from 115 to 80 days with a four-week implementation. Manual touchpoints fell from 20-80 to 2-3 per contract, error rates dropped by roughly 80%, and the organization freed up about 6,000 person-hours a year. Those are not results of faster infrastructure - they are the result of changing the process structure itself.

Through MCP (Model Context Protocol), ProcessOS can also connect AI agents to a process in a controlled way, so orchestration stays with the process engine instead of turning into an agent's "black box" of decisions.

## A migration that also invests in the future

In a follow-up post about moving off a legacy BPMS, Camunda describes a case where the discovery phase was completed 40% faster and at roughly half the cost using ProcessOS Discovery, and within three weeks 40% of the migration work was done - a pace the article describes as 97% faster and 96% cheaper than traditional builds at the same stage.

The important takeaway for a CTO is not just "this is faster." It is that the migration itself builds the foundation for continuous re-engineering afterward, instead of freezing again the moment the project ends - much like what we described in [process orchestration vs. point automation](/en/blog/process-orchestration-vs-automation/).

## What this means for your organization

A 2027 budget that assumes "we'll move the old system to Camunda and be done" misses the significant part. The right question is how the process will get updated in 2028 and 2029 without reopening a year-long project.

In practice, that means budgeting not only for the migration but for the ongoing capability: a discovery tool that runs again when a regulation or product changes, and a governance track that approves changes without stopping the organization. It also requires a clear human-in-the-loop path - ProcessOS accelerates analysis, but process owners are still the ones who sign off on a material change.

At NG Workshop, we see this as the natural continuation of a Process-First approach: AI that works within the process, not instead of it - and not only once, during a migration project, but as a standing capability of the organization.

- The 2027 budget should include that ongoing capability, not just the initial migration cost.
